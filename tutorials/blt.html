How to make a really good BLT

Crisp bacon. Cool lettuce. Juicy tomato. Toast that holds it all together. A great BLT is mostly about getting those textures to play nicely.

Beginner · Makes 1 sandwich · About 20 minutes

Five ingredients. One excellent lunch.

BLT stands for bacon, lettuce and tomato. Bread and mayonnaise bring the trio together. This version uses a skillet for the bacon and a toaster for the bread.

Gather your ingredients

2 slices of bread

3 strips of bacon

2–3 slices of ripe tomato

1–2 lettuce leaves

About 1 tbsp mayonnaise

Black pepper; optional pinch of salt

Equipment: skillet, tongs, toaster, cutting board, knife, spreading knife, plate and paper towel. Wash your hands before starting and after handling raw bacon.

The six-step build

Cook the bacon

Lay 3 strips in a single layer in an unheated skillet. Set it over medium-low heat. Turn occasionally with tongs until cooked through and as crisp as you like, usually about 8–12 minutes; thickness and stove heat change the timing.

Small detail, big difference: If it smokes or browns too fast, lower the heat. Follow your package’s cooking instructions.

Drain and set aside

Move the cooked bacon to a clean plate lined with paper towel. It firms up a little as it cools.

Small detail, big difference: Keep raw-bacon utensils and plates away from the ready-to-eat ingredients; wash them before reuse.

Prep the fresh stuff

Rinse the tomato and lettuce under running water. Dry the lettuce well. Cut 2–3 tomato slices, about ¼ inch thick, on a clean cutting board.

Small detail, big difference: A ripe tomato is the star. If the cut slices are very wet, blot them gently.

Toast, then spread

Toast 2 slices of bread until lightly golden. Let steam escape briefly, then spread about 1 tablespoon of mayonnaise between the two inward-facing sides.

Small detail, big difference: Aim for crisp edges and a little give—not toast so hard it scrapes your mouth.

Build the stack

On one mayo-covered slice, add 1–2 lettuce leaves, tomato slices, a little black pepper, and the bacon. Break the strips to fit. Add a tiny pinch of salt to the tomato if you want.

Small detail, big difference: Bacon is already salty. Lettuce next to the bread helps keep tomato juice from soaking straight in.

Close, cut, crunch

Set the second slice on top, mayo facing in. Hold gently and cut diagonally with a serrated knife. Eat while the toast is crisp.

Small detail, big difference: No need to squash it flat. A clean plate and a napkin finish the job.

The secret is controlling moisture

Dry the lettuce, drain the bacon, and let the toast stop steaming before you assemble. Mayo and lettuce help slow sogginess, but they cannot keep a juicy sandwich crisp forever.

Prep the parts ahead if needed. Build the sandwich when you’re ready to eat.

Three easy fixes

“My sandwich goes soggy.”

Dry the lettuce more thoroughly, blot very wet tomato slices, and avoid assembling far ahead. Let hot toast vent briefly rather than stacking the slices together.

“The bacon is burnt at the edges but soft elsewhere.”

Use gentler heat and a single layer. Turn the strips and move them around as needed; skillets can have hot spots. Do not rely on the clock alone.

“Everything slides out when I bite.”

Use fewer, evenly spread fillings. Break the bacon to fit the bread, avoid a towering pile of tomato, and cut the finished sandwich in half.

Try the two-question check

Why dry lettuce if it’s going into a juicy sandwich?

Water clinging to the leaves adds moisture without adding flavor. Removing it keeps the toast crisp longer and helps the mayo cling.

Does extra-high heat make better crispy bacon?

Not necessarily. It can burn the outside before the fat has time to render. Moderate, steady heat gives you more control; adjust to your stove and bacon.

Your first bite is the final check

You want crunch, a little salt, fresh tomato flavor, and enough mayo to bring it together. Next time, change just one thing: the toast level, the tomato thickness, or the amount of bacon.

That’s the whole recipe: cook, dry, toast, stack, eat.
