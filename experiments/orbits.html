Our assumptions, and what the numbers mean

All centers follow perfect circular paths with the same radius and angular speed around an ideal spherical body. Planes are fixed; there is no drag, precession, maneuvering, or uncertainty. The planet is drawn small for visibility.

Two distinct planes intersect along a line through the center, so their circular paths meet at two opposite points. Polar planes share the poles. Matching the arrival times makes centers coincide; separating those times can help, but nearby approaches also matter.

The full-orbit minimum is calculated analytically for every pair of point trajectories, rather than inferred from the animation's frames. The selected clearance is illustrative and measured as a fraction of orbital radius; it is not a satellite size, an operational safety limit, or a validated constellation design.

A finite train with a gap is an illustration of our packed-ring idea. It does not establish that an arbitrarily large number of finite-size satellites can fit through a gap.
